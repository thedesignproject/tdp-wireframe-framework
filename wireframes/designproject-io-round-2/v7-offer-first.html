Why the thinking behind this version

Most buyers want two answers before they agree to a call: what exactly do I get, and what does it cost? Most studios hide both behind "Book a call", which feels like a sales trap. This version answers them right after the hero. The three plans sit side by side with prices and who each one is for (B). If you can't pick, three quick questions point you to one (C). Then each plan gets its own proof, so the result you read matches the plan you're looking at (D). A short FAQ clears the last doubts about trial, pausing, the team and tools (E), and the call comes last as "help me choose", not "let us pitch you" (F). Trade-off: showing prices this early is clear and honest, but it can scare off big companies or make us look cheap before anyone sees the work.

How to read this board: the big frame on the left is the full homepage, top to bottom. Each section is lettered (A, B, C…) and carries a small dashed tag naming the persuasion principle it relies on. Those principles are explained in plain words on the Overview board. The right column shows the phone version, a section-by-section summary and open questions.
