Why the thinking behind this version

Every studio now calls itself "AI-native", so the words alone prove nothing. What a buyer actually wants from AI is speed: the same product, sooner. This version proves speed with time, not adjectives, building on the "three months to one" idea from the 2 Nov call. Right after the hero, one client says in their own words how much time we saved them (B). Then a set of "time receipts" (C) shows, client by client, how long the work was expected to take next to how long it took with us. Next we show what you'll actually have in hand after 7, 30 and 60 days (D), a few short quotes back it up (E), and the close asks you to ship this quarter while there's still room (F). Trade-off: it's the most convincing version if the numbers are real, and it falls apart without honest time data from each client.

How to read this board: the big frame on the left is the full homepage, top to bottom. Each section is lettered (A, B, C…) and carries a small dashed tag naming the persuasion principle it relies on. Those principles are explained in plain words on the Overview board. The right column shows the phone version, a section-by-section summary and open questions.
