Why the thinking behind this version

People remember stories, not grids of logos. So after the hero, this version tells one client's story from start to finish, like a short article: Imera went from a consumer app to a B2B platform that won a global enterprise customer in three months (B). Four short chapters cover the problem (C), the prototype they could click in the first weeks, which the visitor can click too (D), the weekly shipping (E) and the result (F). A row of other clients shows it wasn't a one-off (G). Only then, once the visitor wants that story for themselves, do we show the three ways to work, with full prices (I), and ask for the call (J). Trade-off: the page leans on one client. If the visitor isn't in B2B or doesn't care about Imera, it takes longer to win them.

How to read this board: the big frame on the left is the full homepage, top to bottom. Each section is lettered (A, B, C…) and carries a small dashed tag naming the persuasion principle it relies on. Those principles are explained in plain words on the round 1 Overview board. The right column shows the phone version, a section-by-section summary and open questions.
