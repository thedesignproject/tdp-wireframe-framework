Why the thinking behind this version

Someone who lands on our homepage is usually already half-interested, but they arrive with the same few worries every buyer has: is an outside team slower than hiring, does "AI" mean cheap work, will they fit in with us, can we even talk across time zones, and what if it goes wrong? This version says those worries out loud, in the order people tend to have them, and answers each one with a fact or a client's own words rather than a promise (B). Once the doubts are handled, a quiet row of client names confirms other teams took the leap (C), the trial terms take away the last risk (D), and the page asks for a call (E). Trade-off: naming a doubt can plant one the visitor didn't have, and if the answers get long, the page reads like an FAQ instead of a pitch.

How to read this board: the big frame on the left is the full homepage, top to bottom. Each section is lettered (A, B, C…) and carries a small dashed tag naming the persuasion principle it relies on. Those principles are explained in plain words on the Overview board. The right column shows the phone version, a section-by-section summary and open questions.
