Why the thinking behind this version

People judge design with their eyes before they read a single number. So this version doesn't ask visitors to trust a claim: it shows them a client's product as it looked before we worked on it, and as it looks now. After the hero, one big before-and-after (B) does the convincing on its own, with a drag handle so the visitor can compare the two. Three smaller pairs (C) show it wasn't a one-off, each with the result it led to underneath. Then clients say it in their own words (D), we say what we do in one line (E), and we ask for the call (F). Trade-off: it's the most convincing page if the pictures are strong, but it needs clients to let us show their old screens, and it can start to feel like a portfolio instead of a studio.

How to read this board: the big frame on the left is the full homepage, top to bottom. Each section is lettered (A, B, C…) and carries a small dashed tag naming the persuasion principle it relies on. Those principles are explained in plain words on the Overview board. The right column shows the phone version, a section-by-section summary and open questions.
