Why the thinking behind this version

Our unfair advantage is that we work in public, with YouTube episodes and open GitHub repos. Most studios only claim they're good; we can show it. So right after the hero, the visitor watches us build a real feature (B), then sees a short strip of proof (C) and gets free tools they can use today (D). Giving something useful first makes people more willing to give something back, in our case a call. Testimonials (E) and the community (F) show that other people are already here. The close (G) offers a call or, for people who aren't ready yet, an email signup. Trade-off: it depends on having one episode good enough to be the centerpiece of the homepage.

How to read this board: the big frame on the left is the full homepage, top to bottom. Each section is lettered (A, B, C…) and carries a small dashed tag naming the persuasion principle it relies on. Those principles are explained in plain words on the Overview board. The right column shows the phone version, a section-by-section summary and open questions.
