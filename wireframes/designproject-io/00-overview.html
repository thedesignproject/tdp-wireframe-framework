Why the thinking behind these five versions

On the 2 Nov call the team agreed on what we are (a design-led, AI-native product studio) but not on the order of the story after the headline. So the hero is frozen, identical in all five versions, and only what follows changes. Each version tests one idea someone raised in the call. To make the choice less about taste, every section is tagged with the persuasion principle it relies on. Those principles come from two classic frameworks: Robert Cialdini's seven principles of influence (what makes people say yes) and Aristotle's three appeals (trust, emotion, logic). Both are explained in plain words at the bottom of this board, followed by a table of which principle each version leans on most.

How to read this board: the five columns are the five versions, read top to bottom like a scroll. The first step (Hero) is solid because it's the same everywhere; the dashed steps are what changes.

Cialdini: seven reasons people say yes

ReciprocityPeople feel they owe you once you've given them something.Free videos, open repos, guides, the 5-minute assessment, before we ask for a call.
Commitment & consistencyOnce someone says a small yes, a bigger yes feels natural.Tiny first steps: click "this is us", take a quiz, join the email list, try a sprint before a full team.
Social proofIf people like me trust them, I can too.Client logos, testimonials, "50+ funded B2B startups".
AuthorityWe believe experts and track records."Acquired by Slack / NVIDIA", hard result numbers, awards.
LikingWe say yes to people we know and like.Real faces, video, the team on camera, plain talk.
ScarcityThings feel more valuable when they're limited."[N] spots open this month", only if it's true.
UnityWe trust people who are "one of us"."Teams like yours", your industry, our builder community.

Aristotle: three ways to persuade

Ethos (trust)Why should I believe them?Who we've worked with, results, open work.
Pathos (emotion)Why should I care?Their pain, real people's words, the feeling of "this is for me".
Logos (logic)Why does it make sense?Numbers, how the model works, prices.

Aristotle also named timing (kairos): "why now?". Every version answers it in the closing section ("ship this quarter").
