Why the thinking behind this version

Nobody knows The Design Project yet, so the page's first job is to make a stranger trust us. On the 2 Nov call the team proposed this exact order (hero, logos and testimonials, results, what we do, YouTube and GitHub, book a call), and this board draws it as the homepage. After the hero, each section adds one more reason to believe us: famous client logos (B), a client in their own words (C), then hard numbers (D). Only once trust is built do we explain what we do (E), because people ignore explanations from a company they don't trust yet. YouTube and GitHub (F) come late, as a bonus for people who want to dig deeper, and the ask comes last (G). Trade-off: it's the safest order, and it looks like most studio websites.

How to read this board: the big frame on the left is the full homepage, top to bottom. Each section is lettered (A, B, C…) and carries a small dashed tag naming the persuasion principle it relies on. Those principles are explained in plain words on the Overview board. The right column shows the phone version, a section-by-section summary and the notes. Each numbered circle has a note: the principles that section pulls, why it sits there, and what the visitor should think by the end of it. In a browser, hover or tap a circle to read its note right there.

All 7 principles present. Weight: ethos 3, logos 2, pathos 2, plus kairos at the close.

Notes what each section is for
  1. 1Authority + unity · Ethos. The headline says who we are in one line; “one team with yours” says we work inside your team, not beside it. It comes first because a stranger decides in seconds whether to stay. Leaves them thinking: “senior people who’d feel like part of my team.”
  2. 2Authority + social proof · Ethos. Slack, NVIDIA, MrBeast and Accel are names the visitor already trusts, so we borrow that trust before they read anything else. It sits right under the headline to back the claim up at once. Leaves them thinking: “products they worked on got bought by companies I know.”
  3. 3Social proof · Ethos. Twelve logos and “50+ funded B2B teams” show this isn’t new for us. One famous name could be luck; a wall of them is a track record, so it comes straight after the hero. Leaves them thinking: “lots of teams like mine already trust them.”
  4. 4Social proof + liking · Pathos. A real person, with a face and a title, saying what the work did for their mission. It follows the logos to turn names into a human voice, and the $100M raise under the quote keeps it from sounding like flattery. Leaves them thinking: “people like me were glad they hired them.”
  5. 5Authority · Logos. Every card leads with a number, not a client name. It comes after the quotes because feelings get people listening and numbers get them believing. Leaves them thinking: “it works, and here’s by how much.”
  6. 6Logos, no Cialdini lever on purpose. Only now do we explain what we do, because people skip a company’s explanation until they trust it. Four plain names, a line each and three concrete deliverables, so visitors spot their own need fast. Leaves them thinking: “I know which one I’d buy.”
  7. 7Reciprocity + liking · Ethos. Free episodes and open repos give something away before we ask for anything, and show the team doing the real work. It sits late, for visitors who want to dig deeper before they talk to us. Leaves them thinking: “they’re generous, and they’re the real thing.”
  8. 8Scarcity + commitment + unity · Pathos + Kairos. “Let’s build” makes it a joint project; three clear steps make the call a small yes; “start within 7 days” and capped spots say why now. It’s last because an ask only lands once trust is built. Ship the spots line only with a real number: fake scarcity undoes everything above. Leaves them thinking: “low risk, and worth doing this month.”
