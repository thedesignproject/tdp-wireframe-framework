Why the thinking behind this version

On the call someone said: "If I don't know the company, I won't read their case study." But people do read what other people say about a company. So this version swaps case studies for a wall of short testimonials right after the hero (B): for a brand nobody knows, many voices are more convincing than one deep story. Numbers (C) then back the voices up, followed by a single sentence about what we do (D), with no process explanation (the call agreed that belongs on its own page). "Teams like you" (E) lets visitors find someone at their stage who already worked with us, and the close (F) invites them to join. Trade-off: it only works with 8–12 real quotes with faces, and we have 3 today.

How to read this board: the big frame on the left is the full homepage, top to bottom. Each section is lettered (A, B, C…) and carries a small dashed tag naming the persuasion principle it relies on. Those principles are explained in plain words on the Overview board. The right column shows the phone version, a section-by-section summary and open questions.
